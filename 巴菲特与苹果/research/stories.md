# 巴菲特与苹果：故事素材与核实记录（stories.md）

Compiled 2026-11-04. Companion to `meeting_quotes.md` (annual-meeting quotes), `letters_apple.md` (shareholder letters) and `13f_apple.md` (Berkshire 13F data) in this folder. Material already verified in those files is cross-referenced here, not repeated in full.

## Labels

- **verified-primary**: wording checked against a transcript, filing or the originating outlet's own report of its interview.
- **verified-secondary**: two independent reputable outlets agree on the wording or the fact.
- **unverified**: only one outlet, the outlets disagree, or I could not reach the original. Do not put these inside quotation marks in narration without checking the original.

## Method notes

- Steady Compounding full-text meeting transcripts were downloaded with curl. Only the 2017, 2023, 2024 and 2025 pages exist; the others returned 404.
- curl gets HTTP 403 from cnbc.com. CNBC pages were read with a fetch tool that returns quotes of 125 characters or less, so long passages are rebuilt from consecutive fragments, marked with ` / ` or `…`.
- SEC EDGAR data was pulled with the User-Agent `research-bot admin@example.com`.
- Xueqiu (雪球) blocks automated reading without a login cookie, and I did not try to get around that. Duan Yongping's Xueqiu posts are therefore quoted from (a) the book 《大道：段永平投资问答录》 (CITIC Press, May 2025; compiled by 赵理亚, edited by 芒格书院), whose dated post excerpts Huxiu reprinted, and (b) news reports. Before broadcast, check each post date against the original Xueqiu post.

### A3. The "apperceptive mass" answer, 2024 annual meeting (Sat 2024-05-04, afternoon session)

**Context:** Stefan Wuernbacher, a shareholder from Hamburg, asked Buffett how he decides when to exit a position. Buffett used the answer to explain how he came to put a lot of money into Apple. Verbatim from the Steady Compounding transcript; key fragments match the CNBC transcript (`meeting_quotes.md` §4, "Bonus"). Bracketed words are the transcriber's.

> "Charlie and I made decisions extremely fast. But in effect, after years of thinking about the parameters that would enable us to make the quick decision when it presented itself. People have speculated on how I've decided to really put a lot of money into Apple … One thing that Charlie and I both learned a lot about was consumer behavior."

> [The Baltimore department store (Hochschild-Kohn) was a mistake that taught them about consumer behaviour; then See's Candy.] "And that sort of background, in a very general way, led up to [our] study of consumer behavior in terms of Apple's products."

> "…while I watched what was happening at the furniture mart, in terms of people leaving the store, even though we were selling Apple products at a price where we weren't even making any money, [they] were just so popular that if we didn't have it, people left the store and went to Best Buy or someplace."

> "But I think the psychologists call this apperceptive mass. But there is something that comes along that takes a whole bunch of observations that you've made and knowledge you have and then crystallizes your thinking into action. [This led to] big action in the case of Apple."

> "And there actually is something, which I don't mean to be mysterious, but I really can't talk about, but it was perfectly legal … And I guess my mind reached what they call apperceptive mass, which I really don't know anything about, but I know the phenomenon when I experience it."

> "There may be some little guy inside the iPhone or something. I have no idea how it works. But I also know what it means. I know what it means to people, and I know how they use it. And I think I know enough about consumer behavior to know that it's one of the great products, maybe the greatest product of all time."

- Label: verified-primary (the Steady Compounding full text; CNBC confirms "one thing that Charlie and I both learned a lot about was consumer behavior", "people left the store and went to Best Buy or someplace", "But I think the psychologists call this apperceptive mass", "Big action, in the case of Apple", "but I really can't talk about").
- Sources: https://steadycompounding.com/transcript/brk-2024/ ; https://buffett.cnbc.com/video/2024/05/06/afternoon-session---2024-meeting.html
- For narration: the story runs consumer behaviour (furniture store → See's → Nebraska Furniture Mart customers walking out to Best Buy) → many observations "crystallize" → one more thing he "can't talk about" → big action. Buffett never said what that one thing was. **Do not speculate in narration.**

### A4. The first purchase in 2016: Todd Combs or Ted Weschler

- Fact: Berkshire's 13F for Q1 2016, filed 2016-05-16, showed a new position of about 9.8 million Apple shares (pre-2020-split), worth about $1.07 billion. Label: verified-secondary (CNBC 2016-05-16; AP/Jakarta Post 2016-05-17; Fortune 2016-05-16). See also `13f_apple.md`.
- Attribution: at the time, CNBC (and Fortune, citing CNBC) reported that Combs and Weschler made the purchase. AP and Reuters wrote that the filing did not say who did. Buffett's general rule, quoted by AP: investments under $1 billion "are likely to be the work of Ted Weschler or Todd Combs." Buffett later confirmed that one of the two managers made the first purchase, but **he never said which one**. CNBC 2017-08-30: "The fellow he was referring to was likely either Todd Combs or Ted Weschler, money managers at Berkshire. But Buffett refused to say which one."
- **The WSJ, May 2016:** later secondary pieces (e.g. an Allocators Asia Substack summary) say "Buffett told the Wall Street Journal that either Combs or Weschler made the investment." I **could not reach the WSJ article** (paywall), so the exact wording is **unverified**. Do not quote it; paraphrase only.
- Sources: https://www.cnbc.com/2016/05/16/buffetts-berkshire-hathaway-takes-new-stake-in-apple.html ; https://fortune.com/2016/05/16/warren-buffett-apple-berkshire/ ; https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2016/05/17/apples-latest-innovation--a-big-stake-for-berkshire-.html ; https://www.cnbc.com/id/104681222?view=story ; 2016 letter text on the managers in `letters_apple.md`.
- Narration-safe wording: "2016年初，伯克希尔首次买入苹果，出手的是巴菲特的两位投资经理之一——托德·库姆斯或特德·韦施勒；巴菲特从未说过是哪一位。"

- Sources: https://9to5mac.com/2020/02/24/the-best-business/ ; https://macdailynews.com/2020/02/24/warren-buffett-apple-is-probably-the-best-business-i-know-in-the-world/ ; https://fortune.com/2020/02/25/warren-buffett-flip-phone-iphone

**"Consumer products company, not tech": there is no verified Buffett sentence with these words.**
- The phrase comes from **reporters' descriptions** (Reuters 2018: Buffett "views Apple more as a consumer products company"; AP likewise) and from **Tim Cook** (CNBC, May 2019), who said Berkshire's investment shows Apple is at heart a consumer company, not a tech company. Reference: https://macdailynews.com/2019/05/06/tim-cook-says-warren-buffetts-investment-shows-that-apple-isnt-really-a-tech-company-anymore/ (unverified wording).
- What Buffett did say (verified-primary, 2018 annual meeting): "Apple has a incredible consumer product which you understand a lot better than I do." And at the 2024 meeting, the whole Apple story is framed as "consumer behavior" (A3).
- Narration-safe wording: "巴菲特把苹果看成一门理解消费者行为的生意，而不是一只科技股" works as narration (no quotation marks). Never present "Apple is a consumer products company" as a Buffett quote.

Other verified Buffett lines (2018–2025) are in `meeting_quotes.md`, including "a better business than any we own" (2023), "an even better business" (2024) and "Tim Cook has made Berkshire a lot more money than I've ever made Berkshire Hathaway" (2025).
