# Buffett on Apple: what he said at annual meetings and in interviews, 2016–2025 (verified quotes)

Compiled 2026-11-03 for the documentary. Companion to `letters_apple.md` and `13f_apple.md`.

## Labels and method

- **verified-primary**: the wording appears in a transcript. The main transcript source is the CNBC Warren Buffett Archive (buffett.cnbc.com, meeting transcripts split into sections). Where possible it was also checked against the independent Steady Compounding transcript.
- **verified-secondary**: there is no transcript, but at least two independent reputable outlets report identical wording.
- **unverified**: one outlet only, or outlets that disagree on the wording, or all reports trace back to the same single source.

How the transcripts were read. `curl` cannot reach buffett.cnbc.com (Akamai returns 403), and the egress proxy blocks web.archive.org. The CNBC transcripts were therefore read with a fetch tool that returns quotes of 125 characters or less. Longer passages are reconstructed below from consecutive fragments, and the joins are marked with ` / `. Each fragment was checked against the full-text Steady Compounding transcript (steadycompounding.com/transcript/brk-YYYY/), downloaded with curl. Where the two transcripts differ, the CNBC wording is used and the difference is noted. Before broadcast, play the CNBC archive video and check the exact syllables.

Nothing inside quotation marks has been paraphrased. Text in [brackets] is an editor's note or an outlet's own insertion, as marked.

## 3. 2023 meeting (Sat 2023-05-06): Apple is "a better business than any we own". Speaker: Warren Buffett

The question cited Professor Aswath Damodaran's view that Apple, at about 35% of the portfolio, is near a "danger zone". CNBC section 24: "Apple is a 'better business' than any Berkshire company" (morning session).

Steady Compounding: "Our railroad is a very good business. It's not remotely as good as Apple's business." The first word, "But" or "It's", differs between the two transcripts. ## 4. 2024 meeting (Sat 2024-05-04): trimming Apple, taxes, cash, "even better business". Speaker: Warren Buffett

The first question of the day: Becky Quick read the 10-Q ("It shows that Berkshire sold another 115 million shares of Apple in this last quarter."), followed by Sherman Lam's question. CNBC morning-session section 4: "Selling some Apple shares now with higher tax rates expected later".

| Quote (verbatim, CNBC transcript) | Label |
|---|---|
| "And I would say that at the end of the year, I would think it extremely likely / that Apple is the largest common stock holding we have." | verified-primary (Steady Compounding adds "now" at the end) |
| "…why we own American Express, which is a wonderful business. We own Coca-Cola, which is a wonderful business. / And we own Apple, which is an even better business." | verified-primary (Steady Compounding agrees) |
| "But and we will own unless something really extraordinary happens we will own Apple, / and American Express, and Coca-Cola when Greg takes over this place." | verified-primary. This is a disfluent transcript. A clean usable cut: "we will own Apple, and American Express, and Coca-Cola when Greg takes over this place." |
| "we will end up unless something dramatically happens that really changes capital allocation / and strategy we will have Apple as our largest investment." | verified-primary. Steady Compounding has "if something dramatically happens", so check the audio. |
| "But I don't mind at all, under current conditions, building the cash position." | verified-primary (CNBC and Steady Compounding agree) |
| "I think when I look at the alternative of what's available in the equity markets, / and I look at the composition of what's going on in the world, we find it quite attractive." | verified-primary. Steady Compounding has "available, the equity markets,". |
| "We don't mind paying taxes at Berkshire, and we are paying a 21% federal rate on the gains we're taking in Apple." | verified-primary for "And we are paying a 21% federal rate on the gains we're taking in Apple." (CNBC). The first clause is from Steady Compounding. |
| "And that rate was 35% not that long ago, and it's been 52% in the past when I've been operating." | verified-primary (Steady Compounding wording, confirmed by CNBC's section summary) |
| "I don't think you'll actually mind the fact that we sold a little Apple this year." | verified-primary (CNBC and Steady Compounding agree) |

- **Correction to the brief:** Buffett did **not** say "Apple is an even better business than American Express or Coca-Cola." The actual words are "We own American Express, which is a wonderful business. We own Coca-Cola, which is a wonderful business. And we own Apple, which is an even better business." Do not put the "than…" version inside quotation marks. Reuters, The Fly and Kiplinger used it only as their own paraphrase.
- **Tax wording:** the brief's "...and the tax ... rates" is not in the transcript. In the transcript, the alternatives line ("I think when I look at the alternative of what's available in the equity markets, and I look at the composition of what's going on in the world, we find it quite attractive.") is a separate sentence from the tax passage. The tax reasoning comes after it (21% now, 35% not long ago, 52% in the past, "higher taxes are quite likely" per CNBC's own section summary).
- Sources: https://buffett.cnbc.com/video/2024/05/06/morning-session---2024-meeting.html (section 4) ; clip https://buffett.cnbc.com/video/2024/05/06/buffett-prospect-of-higher-tax-rate-in-the-future-made-sale-of-apple-shares-more-attractive-now.html ; https://steadycompounding.com/transcript/brk-2024/ ; notes by Prof. David Kass (UMD), https://blog.umd.edu/davidkass/?p=4275 (paraphrased notes, not a transcript)

**Bonus, 2024 meeting, afternoon session, section 5 "'Seeing something that turns on the lightbulb'": the origin story of the Apple purchase.** All verified-primary (CNBC):
- "one thing that Charlie and I both learned a lot about was consumer behavior."
- "if we didn't have it, people left the store and went to Best Buy or someplace." (about Apple products at Nebraska Furniture Mart; the CNBC transcript has "didnt")
- "But I think the psychologists call this apperceptive mass."
- "Big action, in the case of Apple."
- "…which I don't mean to be mysterious, but I really can't talk about…" (Steady Compounding wording. CNBC confirms the fragment "but I really cant talk about.")
- Source: https://buffett.cnbc.com/video/2024/05/06/afternoon-session---2024-meeting.html

Also in the same morning session (section 6, utilities): "I mean, you look at the return on tangible equity at Coca-Cola or American Express or, to really top it off, Apple." verified-primary (CNBC and Steady Compounding).

## 5. 2025 meeting (Sat 2025-05-03). Speaker: Warren Buffett

**Finding: in the transcript searched (Steady Compounding, full text), nobody comments on Berkshire having sold Apple, and nobody credits Todd Combs or Ted Weschler with the Apple purchase.** That covers Buffett, Greg Abel and the questioners. The only "Todd" in the 2025 transcript is GEICO's Todd Combs, in Ajit Jain's answer about GEICO. If the film needs an on-the-record "selling was right/wrong" line, the closest is the 2023 "dumb decision" remark (section 3), which is about the 2020 sales. The other is the 2024 tax rationale (section 4).

What Buffett did say about Apple in 2025 (in his opening remarks):

| Quote (verbatim) | Label |
|---|---|
| "I'm somewhat embarrassed to say that Tim Cook has made Berkshire a lot more money than I've ever made Berkshire Hathaway." | verified-secondary. The Motley Fool (2025-05-14) and the Steady Compounding transcript are identical. Fortune (2025-05-05) inserts "[for]" before "Berkshire Hathaway". |
| "Steve picked Tim to succeed him, and he really made the right decision." | verified-secondary (Fortune, Motley Fool, Steady Compounding) |
| "nobody but Steve could have created Apple, but nobody but Tim could have developed it as he has." | verified-secondary (Fortune, Motley Fool, Steady Compounding) |
| "So on behalf of all of Berkshire, thank you, Tim." | verified-secondary (Fortune; Steady Compounding has no comma before "Tim") |

Buffett has **never publicly named which of the two** made the purchase. |

- Sources: https://www.cnbc.com/id/104681222?view=story (CNBC, "Billionaire Warren Buffett: I've never sold a share of Apple", 2017-08-30) ; https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/reuters/warren-buffett-tells-cnbc-he-has-not-sold-a-share-of-apple-117083001536_1.html ; https://www.industryweek.com/companies-executives/buffett-says-apple-stake-boosted-more-18-billion ; https://blog.umd.edu/davidkass/?p=1998 (notes: "Initially one of Buffett's portfolio managers (Todd Combs or Ted Weschler) bought 10 million shares of Apple (during the first quarter of 2016)") ; https://blog.umd.edu/davidkass/?p=1785 ("92% bought by Buffett, 8% by Combs or Weschler", notes on the 2017-02-27 interview)

- In the 2024 transcript, Sherman Lam's question asks whether "you or your investment managers' views" had changed "since Berkshire first invested in 2016". Buffett's answer does not mention the managers.
